# Sales Fundamentals — worked application workbook (en-IN)

Instructor edition. All companies, people, events and scenario figures are fictional. These tasks use the existing exercise allocation in lessons 2.1, 3.2 and 3.3; they add no course hours. Pilot the timing with learners. Do not use these templates for live outreach without the relevant review.

## Lesson 2.1 — five requests and the tasks behind them

For each statement, write the task, one neutral question and an observable acceptance check. Do not assume purchase intent or fit.

1. Meera at Lake Workshop: “I want to check order status when I am away.”
2. A fictional retailer: “The screen should look more modern.”
3. A fictional services office: “People send different file formats and someone checks every field.”
4. A fictional maintenance firm: “This quote looks expensive, but I have not compared what is included.”
5. A fictional team lead: “We need training without leaving the service desk unattended.”

### Suggested answers and reasons

1. Task: remote status access. Ask how status is checked today. Test authorised access to a fictional order outside the shop. Do not promise savings or untested compatibility.
2. The task is not yet known. Ask which action is difficult on the current screen. Build an acceptance check from that answer rather than imposing an aesthetic preference.
3. Task: compare required fields across supported formats. Ask which fields and formats matter. Test a fictional sample with an agreed format, not an unlimited import promise.
4. Task: compare equivalent scope and cost basis. Ask about term, components, exclusions and review responsibility. A comparison table is more useful than an unauthorised discount.
5. Task: feasible staff training with service coverage. Ask about participants, available time and the participants’ preferred language, if the provider can support it. Agree a plan; do not guarantee zero disruption or unconfirmed language capability.

## Lesson 3.2 — three openings with different sources

Write up to 60 words for each context: identity, truthful source and a small request. Respect buyer channel preferences. No wording in this workbook establishes legal permission for a live campaign.

1. Inbound: Lake Workshop submitted a genuine enquiry about order tracking.
2. Requested introduction: another invented business received your public presentation from Lake Workshop and then asked you to reply by email.
3. Active contact: an organisation-approved process exists for the chosen fictional account and channel, but there was no enquiry, meeting or referral.

### Worked openings

1. “Hello Meera, I’m Alex from the fictional Order Desk team. We received your enquiry about order tracking. May I ask how you check status today?” The request exists and the opening seeks discovery.
2. “Hello, I’m Alex from Order Desk. You asked for an email after receiving our public introduction from Lake Workshop. Which part of your workflow would you like to evaluate?” This does not turn an introduction into a broader endorsement or blanket permission.
3. “Hello, I’m Alex from Order Desk. We work on order tracking for workshops in the supported area. I wanted to check whether this is relevant to your team. If you do not want commercial contact, I will respect that preference.” Only use after channel and process review; never invent prior context.

## Lesson 3.3 — ten process decisions

Identify the principal stage and explain why. Some events span stages; accept a justified alternative that preserves the facts and uncertainty.

1. Research accounts meeting the stated scope and record the information source.
2. Introduce the company and ask whether there is room for a short conversation.
3. Confirm a duplicate-entry problem and ask how an investment would be evaluated.
4. Demonstrate the relevant workflow and invite the buyer to test the agreed condition.
5. Discuss a requested term change with the person authorised to approve it.
6. Complete the applicable approvals and formal agreement.
7. Verify that the two contracted accounts complete the agreed workflow.
8. Receive a stop-contact request during the first conversation.
9. Obtain an accepted invitation before the meeting is held or the need is confirmed.
10. After delivery is confirmed, a client voluntarily shares your public presentation with another interested business.

### Reasoned key

1. Prospecting/research: initial fit is not purchase intent.
2. Opening: a clear purpose is not completed qualification.
3. Qualification/discovery: confirmed problem and decision context do not prove approval.
4. Value presentation: a demonstrated capability is not a guarantee of commercial results.
5. Negotiation: discussion is not an authorised concession until approved.
6. Formal agreement: follow the relevant contracting process and responsibilities.
7. After-sales/delivery: check the actual deliverable rather than treating signature as completion.
8. Respectful closure during the opening: stop and record the preference; refusal is not an objection that must be overcome.
9. Scheduling with qualification gaps: do not record attendance or an approved opportunity without the required evidence.
10. Relationship/voluntary referral: an interested new account can begin another cycle, subject to its own appropriate process. No excessive collection or indiscriminate outreach follows from the referral.

## Self-review

Underline each statement supported by the case. Mark an assumption that remains unconfirmed. Identify one situation where the appropriate decision was not to advance. Revise any answer that depended on a fact the case did not provide.
