## Introduction to selling — 40 min

### 1.1 · What selling means: needs, solutions and value — 10 min

**Objective**
List two confirmed facts, two unknowns, a neutral discovery question and a conditional value hypothesis.

**Instructor script — pending recording**
Imagine you are speaking with Meera, who runs the fictional Lake Workshop in Pune. She says she needs an app. Do not assume the app is the problem to solve. Ask what her team needs to do and what gets in the way. Perhaps staff need to check order status without calling the front desk. That gives us a task to examine. A feature becomes useful only in a buyer’s circumstances; it does not guarantee business growth. Pause and write one question that could disprove your initial sales hypothesis.

**Supporting text**
Separate a feature, a possible benefit and buyer-confirmed value. A dashboard is a feature. Bringing orders together is a possible benefit. Whether that helps enough to justify cost and implementation depends on the buyer. The alternative may be an existing process rather than another vendor. Record current situation, confirmed difficulty, consequence and desired change. Mark an untested statement as a hypothesis. Do not estimate savings unless the inputs and assumptions are available, and do not present potential savings as guaranteed cash or income.

**Fictional case**
Lake Workshop uses paper and messages. Meera wants to check order status remotely. Budget, frequency of errors and migration effort are unknown.

**Exercise**
1. List two confirmed facts, two unknowns, a neutral discovery question and a conditional value hypothesis.

**Suggested answer — instructor**
Facts: paper/messages and desire for remote status access. Unknowns: budget and migration effort. Ask how status is checked today. Hypothesis: a shared view may reduce the need to call the desk; validate that use.

The answer identifies what the buyer actually reported, keeps unmeasured impact separate and tests a conditional benefit rather than guaranteeing it.

**Check your understanding**
The workshop reports difficulty, but you have not confirmed its cause. What next?

1. Send a contract.
2. Investigate a recent example before recommending.
3. Guarantee increased sales.

Answer: 2. A recent example tests the cause. The reported difficulty is not evidence that your product fits.

### 1.2 · Products, services and integrated solutions — 10 min

**Objective**

**Instructor script — pending recording**

Separate access to the product from setup, training and migration. Ask what training arrangement is feasible, then check what the fictional offer actually includes. **Supporting text**

**Fictional case**

**Exercise**
1. Identify the components, explain one exclusion and write an acceptance condition for setup.

**Suggested answer — instructor**
Product access plus setup/training services. Do not promise Marathi training or paper migration. Verify the two test accounts using the agreed workflow and confirm the training arrangement separately.

The acceptance test is limited to a deliverable that can be observed. It does not expand setup into migration, language support or a financial guarantee.

**Check your understanding**
Which statement is a verifiable setup commitment?

1. Revenue will double.
2. Every employee will love the product.
3. Two accounts will complete the agreed test workflow.

Answer: 3. The test checks delivery against scope. Future revenue and universal approval are not guaranteed setup outcomes.

### 1.3 · B2B and B2C buying contexts — 10 min

**Objective**

**Instructor script — pending recording**
A purchase by a workshop and a purchase by a person for home use may involve the same device and different expectations. Do not assume a job title reveals the whole decision process, or that a family-owned business has no formal approvals. Ask who will use, evaluate and authorise the purchase. **Supporting text**
B2B and B2C distinguish buying relationships, not fixed personalities. A business buyer can have emotional concerns; an individual can use a detailed comparison. Map users, technical or operational evaluators, economic approvers and contracting responsibilities. One person may hold several roles. Source S1 is a historical US retail estimate, not an India market statistic and not a conversion benchmark. Its published edition reported a 2024 online retail share of 16.1% and carries a later-revision notice. Check coverage and edition before drawing a conclusion.

**Fictional case**
Lake’s supervisor checks daily use; Meera approves spending; invoicing and contract review responsibilities are unknown.

**Exercise**
1. Map three buying roles and write the question needed to identify the contracting process. Explain why a retail share does not predict a deal outcome.

**Suggested answer — instructor**
Operational evaluator: supervisor; economic approver: Meera; contracting/invoice reviewers: unknown. Ask who must review the scope, invoice requirements and agreement. US retail share cannot predict this Indian workshop’s purchase.

Mapping use, evaluation and approval helps identify missing contracting responsibilities. An aggregate US retail measure cannot predict the outcome of a particular account.

**Check your understanding**
Does an online order automatically identify a consumer purchase?

1. No; buyer and use context still matter.
2. Yes; all website orders are B2C.
3. Yes; B2B requires an in-person visit.

Answer: 1. A business can order online. Channel does not identify the participants, purpose or approval process.

**Supporting text**

**Fictional case**

Demo requested; purchase and terms not approved; confirm participants and purpose before the demo. Track accepted qualified handoffs divided by submitted handoffs, with the acceptance definition and period stated.

The revised handoff reports an observable request and preserves gaps. A quality measure needs both a defined outcome and the denominator against which it is reviewed.

**Check your understanding**

## Understanding the buyer — 45 min

### 2.1 · Needs, preferences, difficulties and expectations — 11 min

**Objective**
Identify one preference, one need and one expectation. Write a question for each and mark one condition you cannot promise.

**Instructor script — pending recording**
Meera says she wants an app, but the underlying task is checking order status while away from the workshop. The preference for an app and the need for access are related but not identical. She also expects staff training that fits their working day. Ask which conditions are essential, which are preferences and what needs checking. Do not infer budget or willingness to change from the city, business size or language. Pause and write an observable success condition for the trial.

**Supporting text**
A need concerns what must be addressed; a preference describes a desired form; a difficulty is an experienced obstacle; an expectation is a condition against which the buyer may judge delivery. These categories overlap and should help the conversation rather than diagnose the person. Ask for a recent example and an acceptable improvement. Not every impact must be monetised. If using a calculation, distinguish a hypothetical estimate from observed results and explain assumptions, implementation costs and what may not be recoverable as cash.

**Fictional case**
Lake wants remote status access, a mobile-friendly experience and training at an agreed time; the provider’s language capability is unknown.

**Exercise**
1. Identify one preference, one need and one expectation. Write a question for each and mark one condition you cannot promise.

**Suggested answer — instructor**
Mobile-friendly form: preference. Remote status access: need to confirm. Agreed training arrangement: expectation. Ask who needs training and their preferred language; do not promise an unconfirmed training language.

The answer separates form preference from an operational need and an implementation expectation. It marks an unverified condition instead of promising an effortless change.

**Check your understanding**
The buyer wants a modern system. Does that establish a need for AI?

1. Yes; modern always means AI.
2. Only if there is a discount.
3. No; clarify the task and success condition.

Answer: 3. A vague preference is not a requirement for a specific technology. Discover the task first.

### 2.2 · Recognising a potential buyer profile — 11 min

**Objective**
Classify A–D, justify with product scope and contact restrictions, and name the minimum data needed.

**Instructor script — pending recording**
Lake appears relevant because it has one location and three users, not because Meera belongs to a demographic group. The offer’s scope determines what we can support. Use sector, operational needs, service region and technical requirements. Do not collect Aadhaar, health details or family information for an initial sales discussion. If a field is not needed for this decision, leave it out. Public availability does not settle every data-protection question. Pause and specify the minimum fields your fictional account list needs.

**Supporting text**
An ideal customer profile describes account characteristics associated with potential fit. A buyer persona concerns a role and context in the decision. Neither proves purchase intent. Separate required fit conditions from tentative prioritisation signals and explicit exclusions. Record source, date and uncertainty. Unknown is a valid status. Privacy and outreach approval are separate checks from product fit; route legal questions to the responsible team. The India DPDP framework and relevant channel rules require review of applicability and effective provisions, not a claim that one generic script establishes compliance.

**Fictional case**
A: one location in the supported area, three users. B: multiple-location integration required. C: user count unknown. D: one location, explicit no-contact preference.

**Exercise**
1. Classify A–D, justify with product scope and contact restrictions, and name the minimum data needed.

**Suggested answer — instructor**
A: investigate need; B: outside scope; C: clarify user count through an approved process; D: no outreach. Keep only relevant account and process facts, with source/date and restrictions.

The classification uses stated scope and distinguishes unknown from incompatible. It also recognises that a matching account can still be excluded from contact.

**Check your understanding**
An account matches the product but has asked for no contact. What applies?

1. Respect the restriction rather than add it to outreach.
2. Product fit cancels the stop request.
3. Try a different sender.

Answer: 1. Product fit and contact permission are separate decisions. Do not evade the restriction.

### 2.3 · Open, closed and probing questions — 11 min

**Objective**
Write one open question, one probe and one confirmation. Identify the unsupported inference.

**Instructor script — pending recording**
In a short Lake conversation, one clear question is often easier to answer than a long list of qualification fields. Ask how orders are recorded, then explore one example. Confirm details without converting “sometimes” into “every day”. If discussing budget, explain why the question matters and accept that the contact may need to check the process. Do not turn uncertainty into pressure. Pause and write an open question, a probe and a confirmation that still allow Meera to correct you.

**Supporting text**
Use question types for different purposes. Open questions uncover context; probes develop a specific point; closed questions verify facts. A neutral sequence is context → recent example → consequence → confirmation. Questions about authority, resources or timing should help assess a path, not create an interrogation. Avoid leading phrasing, false alternatives and implied threats. If an answer is unavailable, record the gap. Do not ask for sensitive documents to make a basic qualification form appear complete.

**Fictional case**
Meera says checking orders is “inconvenient”; the rep records “severe daily revenue loss”.

**Exercise**
1. Write one open question, one probe and one confirmation. Identify the unsupported inference.

**Suggested answer — instructor**
How do you check status today? Can you describe the last inconvenient check? Is remote access the task you want to test? Severe revenue loss was never confirmed.

The question sequence gathers context and a concrete episode, then invites correction. It leaves frequency and impact unconfirmed when the buyer has not measured them.

**Check your understanding**
Which question avoids assuming a financial loss?

1. How much money do you lose every day?
2. What happens when an order cannot be located?
3. Do you agree your team is failing?

Answer: 2. The second question explores a consequence without imposing amount, frequency or blame.

### 2.4 · Active listening and professional communication — 12 min

**Supporting text**

Prefer observable notes to character judgements: “wants to verify compatibility” rather than “difficult buyer”. Do not score intelligence or interest from accent, response speed or cultural assumptions. Use only fictional data in role-play. **Fictional case**

**Exercise**
1. Paraphrase faithfully, choose the immediate action and perform the formative role-play.

**Suggested answer — instructor**
Future review is uncertain; the WhatsApp stop request is explicit. Stop the relevant commercial contact and record the restriction without switching channels to evade it.

A faithful summary retains both uncertain future interest and a definite present restriction. The chosen action respects the restriction instead of upgrading politeness into consent.

**Check your understanding**

**Instructor script — pending recording**

**Suggested answer — instructor**

**Check your understanding**

## Knowledge check — answer-key edition

20 questions · 16 correct for 80% · 12 minutes included in lesson 3.5.

### 1. Lake Workshop reports repeated entries but the cause is unknown. What should the rep do before recommending a tool?

1. Ask where duplication occurs and verify the underlying task.
2. Guarantee lower costs to create interest.
3. Send an agreement because every workshop needs software.

Answer: 1. Discovery checks whether the offer fits. A reported difficulty does not establish its cause or guarantee savings.

### 2. Which statement is a testable value hypothesis?

1. The tool will eliminate all errors.
2. We can test whether a shared record helps your team check duplicate entries.
3. Every buyer will grow revenue.

Answer: 2. The second statement proposes a contextual benefit to test. The other statements promise results without evidence.

### 3. The buyer expects historical paper records to be entered, but that work is excluded. What is the appropriate action?

1. Let the buyer assume it is included.
2. Blame the buyer for misunderstanding.
3. Clarify the exclusion before agreement and evaluate the separate need.

Answer: 3. Scope must be clear before commitment. Silence and blame do not resolve the expectation.

### 4. Which condition checks delivery of account setup?

1. Two test accounts complete the workflow defined in the agreed scope.
2. The buyer necessarily doubles revenue.
3. No user ever needs support.

Answer: 1. An observed workflow checks a specified deliverable. Future revenue and absence of all questions are not setup guarantees.

### 5. A workshop orders equipment on a website for business use. Does the online channel alone make this B2C?

1. Yes, every website purchase is B2C.
2. No, the parties and use context still matter.
3. Yes, B2B requires a site visit.

Answer: 2. Channel does not determine buying context. Businesses can buy online.

### 6. A supervisor tests the workflow and the owner approves spending. Which question remains relevant?

1. What is the supervisor’s personal income?
2. Which person is easiest to pressure?
3. Who reviews terms and formalises the agreement?

Answer: 3. The contracting path is relevant to the decision; personal income and pressure are not a substitute for understanding it.

### 7. A colleague hands over a record labelled “hot lead”. What would improve it?

1. Confirmed problem, participants, unknowns and a buyer-accepted next step.
2. More enthusiastic punctuation.
3. Treating every assumption as confirmed.

Answer: 1. A factual handoff enables continuity. A label or artificial certainty does not.

### 8. The only buyer statement is “we want something modern”. Which response is neutral?

1. You mean AI, correct?
2. What would you like to do that is difficult today?
3. How much money do you lose without our product?

Answer: 2. The neutral question explores the task without imposing a technology or financial loss.

### 9. The offer supports one location and up to five users. The prospect requires ten connected locations. What is the correct initial assessment?

1. Qualified, because larger firms always buy.
2. Qualified if the contact is friendly.
3. Outside the stated scope; check an authorised alternative before promising fit.

Answer: 3. Product scope limits fit. Size and friendliness do not solve the operational mismatch.

### 10. A business email address appears on a public page. What follows?

1. Record the source; any use still needs the applicable purpose, privacy and channel checks.
2. It can be used for any campaign indefinitely.
3. It can be combined with sensitive personal data to pressure the buyer.

Answer: 1. A public source is not blanket permission for every purpose. The organisation must use its approved procedures.

### 11. Which sequence avoids a premature conclusion?

1. How much are you losing? Why is your team failing? Will you buy today?
2. How does it work now? Can you describe a recent example? Have I understood the difficulty correctly?
3. Do you agree our product is the best?

Answer: 2. The second sequence explores context, an example and confirmation. The alternatives lead or pressure the buyer.

### 12. The buyer says they may review the offer later but wants marketing messages stopped now. What is a faithful record?

1. Purchase confirmed for later.
2. Continue weekly reminders.
3. Future interest uncertain; stop the requested contact and record the preference through the approved process.

Answer: 3. Possible future interest is not a commitment and does not cancel an explicit stop request.

### 13. An account fits the product but has a recorded no-contact request. What should you do?

1. Keep it out of the outreach sequence and respect the restriction.
2. Use another sender to test the refusal.
3. Ask a colleague to contact the same person instead.

Answer: 1. Fit does not override a stop request. Changing the sender to evade it is inappropriate.

### 14. Which opening invents context?

1. Replying to your enquiry, when an enquiry exists.
2. Following our meeting, when no meeting occurred.
3. I am from the supplier and would like to check relevance, through an approved channel.

Answer: 2. Inventing a meeting creates a false relationship. Identity and context must be truthful.

### 15. The contact confirms a relevant problem but does not know the funding process. How should that field be recorded?

1. Approved, because the problem is important.
2. Zero, because no amount was given.
3. Unknown; explore the approval process at an appropriate point.

Answer: 3. Unknown funding is neither approved nor zero. Preserve uncertainty rather than filling a field by assumption.

### 16. The buyer asks for a discount outside your authority. What is appropriate?

1. Clarify the constraint and consult the authorised owner without promising approval.
2. Grant it first and ask for approval later.
3. Promise that the discount guarantees financial results.

Answer: 1. Delegated authority limits commitments. Consultation preserves both trust and a valid negotiation.

### 17. One account cannot access the service. The rep wants to publish a success story. What should happen first?

1. Require three referrals.
2. Route the access issue and confirm actual delivery before declaring success.
3. Publish a testimonial without checking permission.

Answer: 2. Delivery has not been confirmed while the issue remains unresolved. Referrals and publicity do not replace support.

### 18. A fictional RYCS exercise contains no payment amount or release conditions. Can it establish your earnings?

1. Yes, it represents every live campaign.
2. Yes, multiply the number of contacts by any example price.
3. No, actual opportunity terms and valid activity determine the applicable conditions.

Answer: 3. A learning example does not create remuneration terms, availability or a promise of income.

### 19. A task requires a meeting that was held. The only evidence is an accepted invitation. What does it show by itself?

1. Scheduling acceptance, not confirmed attendance.
2. Attendance by every invitee.
3. Automatic approval of payment.

Answer: 1. Scheduling, attendance and review are distinct events. Evidence must support the specific claim.

### 20. A review says the evidence shows only scheduling. You know the meeting was postponed. What is correct?

1. Alter the screenshot to imply attendance.
2. Acknowledge the fact and correct the status through the available process, preserving record integrity.
3. Rename the same attachment as completed meeting.

Answer: 2. A correction must reflect the event. A changed image or title cannot establish an activity that did not happen.
